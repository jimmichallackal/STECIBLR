STECI History

St. Thomas Evangelical Church of India (STECI) originated from the Mar Thoma Syrian Church with the objective of reconstituting the church according to its founding principles. The STECI was launched at Thymala, Manjadi, Thiruvalla, Kerala, India on Jan 26, 1961.

The Malankara Mar Thoma Syrian Church formally came into existence in 1889 after losing the ‘Seminary Case’ or Royal Court Case against the Jacobite Metropolitan Mar Dionysius. The rift with the Jacobite Metropolitan began after 12 clergymen of the reformed faction of the church under the leadership of Abraham Malpan declared opposition to 24 non-biblical evil practices in the Malankara Church on 5th Sept. 1836. This development was the result of a great reformation that was triggered by the Church Missionary Society and the reformation movement led by Abraham Malpan and Kaithayil Gee Varghese Malpan in 1826.

Based on Biblical teachings, they gave up beliefs and practices contrary to the Word of God that was proclaimed by St. Thomas (one of the Apostles of Jesus Christ who landed in Cranganore, near Cochin in 52 A.D. according to tradition). Within four decades of the formation of the Mar Thoma Church, led by a section of leadership, the Mar Thoma Church started deviating from its reformed faith, doctrines and practices, and became lenient to non-Biblical practices.

Therefore, another Reformist Forum was started by Mr. K. N. Daniel, a lay theologian and activist in 1929. Abraham Mar Thoma Metropolitan was supportive of him until his demise in 1947. However, the drifting from reformist positions continued. It is in this context that on October 29, 1952, the Pathyopadesa Samithi, Forum to Defend Sound Doctrine, was formed. Under the able leadership of Mr. K. N. Daniel, they raised their voices within the church against the drifting away of faith for about a decade.

When this became futile and impossible, the reformed section of the Mar Thoma Church decided to reconstitute the Mother Church under the name St. Thomas Evangelical Church of India in 1961 to retain and continue the reformation. These facts are mentioned in the preamble of the constitution of the STECI.

🌿The History of Bangalore Parish (1964–2025)

I. Humble Beginnings (1964–1979)


The Bangalore Parish traces its origin to the deep desire of Sabha members to join together in fellowship wherever they were placed. This passion gave birth to a prayer group in 1964 at Berian Bible College, initiated by Mr. Rajan Abraham and Mr. Varghese Tenali. Soon, they were joined by Mr. P.V. Alexander, Mr. M.J. Thomas, Mr. P.D. Varghese, and Mr. M.J. Mathai—remembered today as the Founding Pillars of the parish.
For fifteen years, worship was nomadic, moving across:
• India Crusade Workers Hostel under Mr. P.K. Koruth’s leadership
• Hindustan Congregation Parish Hall at Infantry Road (Vishranthi Nilayam Campus)
• Residences of members such as Mr. V.M. Mammen and Dr. M.M. Ninan
In 1975, land was purchased at Lingarajapuram, laying the foundation for a permanent sanctuary.

II. The First Church Building (1980–2003)


Under Rev. T.C. George, the parish constructed its first church building at Lingarajapuram—the first STECI church in Bangalore City. On September 21,
1980, Bishop T.C. Cherian consecrated the sanctuary, marking a historic milestone.
The parish matured into a hub of faith:
• During Rev. Thomas Abraham’s tenure, in 1984 the parish hosted the Bahya Kerala Diocese Conference for the first time.
• Nurturing the Young: Rev. Thomas Varghese recognized that a church without children is a church without a future. He established the Sunday School sowing the seeds of scripture in young minds.
• In 1992, the Parish hosted the Bahya Kerala Diocese conference 2nd time under the guidance of Rev. Thomas Varghese.
• Continuing the passion to bring the believers from Bahya Kerala Diocese, parish hosted the conference on third time in 2004 lead by Rev. K. S. James.
This era cemented Bangalore Parish as a spiritual anchor for diaspora believers.

III. Expansion and New Foundations (2004–2007)


As Bangalore grew into a global IT hub, the parish expanded. Under Rev. P.K. Koruth, members purchased land at Horamavu Agara.
Key milestones:
• October 6, 2005: Foundation stone laid by Rev. P.K. Koruth
• 2006: Basement dedicated by Bishop Dr. M.K. Koshy
• March 24, 2007: New church consecrated by Presiding Bishop Most Rev. T.C. Cherian
Mission initiatives flourished:
• Karnataka Jyothi (2006): Rev. Biju Thomas initiated outreach in Ambedkar Nagar along with Mission Interest Group, transforming prayer meetings into a worship group.
• 2007: A parsonage was constructed under Rev. Thomas Tottathil.

IV. Growth and New Initiatives (2008–2025)


The parish continued to evolve with vision and compassion:
• 2008: Families branched out to form Koramangala Parish.
• 2009: English worship service introduced under Rev. P.M. Joseph, serving the younger generation.
• 2014: Hosted the BKD Conference under Rev. Shaji Alexander.
• September 3, 2021: Officially registered as a Public Religious and Charitable Trust.
• July 14, 2024: New prayer hall dedicated in Bellary mission field under Rev. Laji Varughese.
• August 4, 2024: Utility complex and parking facilities inaugurated by Rev Laji Varughese.
• Participation in the Sabha’s Urban Mission, leading to the formation of STECI City Serve Fellowship (CSF). On February 21, 2025, CSF officially recognized by the Church Council under Rev. Varghese John.
• The year 2025 also saw a physical "rebirth." Under Rev. Laji Varughese, the church underwent a significant renovation. The interiors were redesigned to reflect the beauty and sanctity of God’s presence. On December 21, 2025, the renovated building was rededicated, blending the heritage of the past with the aesthetics of the future.

✨ Legacy and Vision From six believers in a Bible college room to a thriving, multi-lingual parish with missions in Bellary and beyond, the Bangalore Parish stands as a living testimony to faith, fellowship, and perseverance.
It is not merely a record of land and buildings, but a spiritual epic—proof that “The Lord has done great things for us, and we are filled with joy” (Psalm 126:3). Today, the parish shines as a beacon of hope, carrying forward the legacy of its pioneers into a purposeful future.
